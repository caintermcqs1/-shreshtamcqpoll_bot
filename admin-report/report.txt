<b>[Group 1]</b>
🧠 <b>CONCEPT OF THE DAY – Group 1</b>
📘 <b>Advanced Accounting</b>

<b>AS 22 – Deferred tax</b>

• <b>Timing differences</b> arise in one period and reverse later (e.g. depreciation rates differ between books and tax). They create deferred tax.
• <b>Permanent differences</b> never reverse (e.g. a penalty disallowed for tax). No deferred tax on them.
• <b>DTA</b> is recognised only if there is reasonable certainty of future taxable income. For unabsorbed depreciation or carried-forward losses, there must be <b>virtual certainty</b> supported by convincing evidence.
• <b>Measured</b> using tax rates enacted or substantively enacted at the balance sheet date. Deferred tax is NOT discounted.
• <b>Tax holiday (e.g. 80-IA):</b> no deferred tax for timing differences that reverse within the holiday period.

📝 <b>Exam tip:</b> Re-assess unrecognised DTA at every balance sheet date – it can be recognised later when certainty arrives.

<b>[Group 2]</b>
🧠 <b>CONCEPT OF THE DAY – Group 2</b>
📘 <b>Financial Management</b>

<b>NPV vs IRR – when they disagree</b>

• For <b>independent</b> projects with normal cash flows, NPV and IRR give the same accept/reject decision.
• <b>Conflicts</b> arise for mutually exclusive projects due to: size disparity, different timing of cash flows, or unequal lives.
• <b>Reinvestment assumption:</b> NPV assumes reinvestment at the cost of capital; IRR assumes reinvestment at the IRR itself – usually unrealistic.
• <b>Non-conventional cash flows</b> (signs change more than once) can give multiple IRRs or none.
• In a conflict, <b>prefer NPV</b> – it measures the absolute increase in wealth. MIRR fixes the reinvestment problem of IRR.

📝 <b>Exam tip:</b> For unequal lives, use the equivalent annual annuity (EAA) or replacement chain before comparing NPVs.

🧠 <b>CONCEPT OF THE DAY – Group 1</b>
📘 <b>Taxation – GST</b>

<b>Sec 17(5) – Blocked credits</b>

• <b>Motor vehicles</b> for transport of persons with seating capacity of 13 or less (including the driver) – blocked, except when used for further supply of such vehicles, transport of passengers, or training on driving.
• <b>Food, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic surgery, life and health insurance</b> – blocked, unless used for an outward supply of the same category or obligatory under a law for employers.
• <b>Club membership, health and fitness centre, travel benefits</b> to employees on vacation (LTC) – blocked.
• <b>Construction of immovable property</b> (other than plant and machinery) on own account, including works contract services – blocked, even if used in business.
• <b>Goods lost, stolen, destroyed, written off, or given as gifts or free samples</b>, and goods or services for personal consumption – blocked.

📝 <b>Exam tip:</b> Plant and machinery is NOT blocked – but land, buildings and civil structures (and pipelines/telecom towers outside the factory) are not 'plant and machinery'.

<b>[Group 2]</b>
🧠 <b>CONCEPT OF THE DAY – Group 2</b>
📘 <b>Cost &amp; Management Accounting</b>

<b>Activity Based Costing (ABC)</b>

• <b>Idea:</b> activities consume resources and products consume activities – so overheads are traced through activities.
• <b>Steps:</b> identify activities → build cost pools → choose a cost driver for each → compute driver rates → charge products by drivers used.
• <b>Cost hierarchy:</b> unit-level, batch-level, product-sustaining and facility-sustaining activities.
• <b>Drivers:</b> transaction drivers (number of set-ups, orders) and duration drivers (set-up hours, inspection hours).
• <b>Insight:</b> traditional absorption usually UNDER-costs low-volume, complex products and OVER-costs high-volume simple ones.

📝 <b>Exam tip:</b> ABC helps most when overheads are a large share of cost and products differ widely in complexity.

<b>📅 Wed 2 Dec – CA Inter Concept of the Day</b>

<b>[Group 1]</b>
🧠 <b>CONCEPT OF THE DAY – Group 1</b>
📘 <b>Corporate &amp; Other Laws</b>

<b>Dividend – Sec 123 and the payment timeline</b>

• <b>Source:</b> profits of the current year or previous years, after providing for depreciation, or money provided by the Government for payment of dividend under its guarantee.
• <b>Out of free reserves</b> when profits are inadequate – only as per the prescribed rules (previous losses and depreciation must be set off first).
• <b>Interim dividend:</b> declared by the Board. <b>Final dividend:</b> recommended by the Board, declared at the AGM.
• <b>Deposit</b> the dividend amount in a separate bank account within <b>5 days</b> of declaration. Pay within <b>30 days</b> (Sec 127).
• <b>Unpaid dividend</b> → Unpaid Dividend Account within 7 days after the 30 days (Sec 124). Unclaimed for 7 years → transferred to IEPF.

📝 <b>Exam tip:</b> Dividend is payable only in cash – capitalising profits as bonus shares is not a dividend.

<b>[Group 2]</b>
🧠 <b>CONCEPT OF THE DAY – Group 2</b>
📘 <b>Auditing &amp; Ethics</b>

<b>SA 705 – Which modified opinion?</b>

• <b>Qualified opinion:</b> misstatements are material but NOT pervasive, or the auditor cannot get sufficient evidence and the possible effects are material but not pervasive.
• <b>Adverse opinion:</b> misstatements are both material AND pervasive.
• <b>Disclaimer of opinion:</b> the auditor cannot get sufficient appropriate evidence and the possible effects could be both material and pervasive.
• <b>Pervasive</b> = not confined to specific elements, or if confined, a substantial part of the FS, or fundamental to users' understanding of the disclosures.
• The report includes a <b>'Basis for Qualified/Adverse Opinion'</b> or <b>'Basis for Disclaimer of Opinion'</b> paragraph describing the matter.

📝 <b>Exam tip:</b> Two questions decide it: Is it a misstatement or a lack of evidence? Is it pervasive or not?

<b>📅 Thu 3 Dec – CA Inter Concept of the Day</b>

<b>[Group 1]</b>
🧠 <b>CONCEPT OF THE DAY – Group 1</b>
📘 <b>Advanced Accounting</b>

<b>Buy-back of shares – Sec 68, 69 and 70</b>

• <b>Sources:</b> free reserves, securities premium, or proceeds of an issue of shares or securities (but NOT from an earlier issue of the same kind).
• <b>Limits:</b> Board resolution up to 10% of paid-up capital + free reserves; special resolution up to 25%. For equity shares, the buy-back in a year cannot exceed 25% of paid-up equity capital.
• <b>Debt-equity</b> after buy-back must not exceed 2:1 (secured and unsecured debt vs paid-up capital + free reserves).
• <b>Sec 69:</b> when bought back out of free reserves or securities premium, transfer an amount equal to the nominal value bought back to the Capital Redemption Reserve.
• <b>Timing:</b> complete within 1 year of the resolution; no fresh issue of the same kind for 6 months (except bonus and existing obligations); 1 year gap between two buy-backs.

📝 <b>Exam tip:</b> Run all three tests (shares outstanding, resources, debt-equity) – the maximum buy-back is the LOWEST of the three.

🎯 <i>Understand it once, and you won't need to mug it up.</i>

<b>[Group 2]</b>
🧠 <b>CONCEPT OF THE DAY – Group 2</b>
📘 <b>Strategic Management</b>

<b>Porter's value chain</b>

• <b>Value chain</b> splits a firm into activities that create value, so the source of competitive advantage can be found.
• <b>Primary activities:</b> inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, and service.
• <b>Support activities:</b> firm infrastructure, human resource management, technology development and procurement.
• <b>Margin</b> = value created for customers minus the cost of performing the activities.
• <b>Linkages</b> between activities (e.g. better procurement reducing defects in operations) are often a stronger advantage than any single activity.

📝 <b>Exam tip:</b> In a case, map each fact to one activity – then say whether it supports cost leadership or differentiation.

🎯 <i>Understand it once, and you won't need to mug it up.</i>

📘 <b>Taxation – Income Tax</b>

<b>New tax regime – Sec 115BAC for individuals (A.Y. 2026-27)</b>

• <b>Slabs:</b> up to ₹4 lakh nil; ₹4–8 lakh 5%; ₹8–12 lakh 10%; ₹12–16 lakh 15%; ₹16–20 lakh 20%; ₹20–24 lakh 25%; above ₹24 lakh 30%.
• <b>Rebate u/s 87A:</b> up to ₹60,000 where total income does not exceed ₹12 lakh (not available on tax on special-rate income like Sec 111A/112A), with marginal relief just above ₹12 lakh.
• <b>Allowed:</b> standard deduction ₹75,000; family pension deduction ₹25,000; Sec 80CCD(2) employer's NPS contribution; Sec 80CCH; Sec 80JJAA.

• <b>Default regime:</b> applies unless the individual opts out. Without business income – choose every year in the return. With business income – opt out by Form 10-IEA, with only one chance to come back.

📝 <b>Exam tip:</b> Always compute the rebate before cess – health and education cess at 4% is charged on tax AFTER the rebate.

<b>[Group 2]</b>
🧠 <b>CONCEPT OF THE DAY – Group 2</b>
📘 <b>Cost &amp; Management Accounting</b>

<b>Joint products and by-products</b>

• <b>Joint products:</b> two or more products of significant relative value produced at the same time from the same process.
• <b>By-product:</b> a product of relatively small value produced incidentally with the main product.
• <b>Split-off point:</b> the stage where joint products become separately identifiable. Costs up to it are joint costs.
• <b>Apportioning joint costs:</b> physical units method, sales value at split-off, net realisable value method, or constant gross margin method.
• <b>Further processing decision:</b> process further only if incremental revenue exceeds incremental cost after split-off. Joint costs are irrelevant to this decision.

📝 <b>Exam tip:</b> By-product income can be credited to the main product's cost, shown as other income, or handled by the reverse cost method – state which one you use.

<b>📅 Sat 5 Dec – CA Inter Concept of the Day</b>

<b>[Group 1]</b>
🧠 <b>CONCEPT OF THE DAY – Group 1</b>
📘 <b>Taxation – GST</b>

<b>Composition levy – Sec 10</b>

• <b>Eligibility:</b> aggregate turnover in the preceding FY up to ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh in certain special category States).
• <b>Rates (CGST + SGST):</b> manufacturers 1%, traders 1% (on turnover of taxable supplies), restaurants not serving alcohol 5%.
• <b>Services:</b> a composition supplier of goods may also supply services up to 10% of turnover or ₹5 lakh, whichever is higher. A pure service provider can use Sec 10(2A) – turnover up to ₹50 lakh at 6%.
• <b>Not allowed:</b> inter-State outward supplies, non-taxable goods, notified goods (e.g. ice cream, pan masala, tobacco), casual and non-resident taxable persons.
• <b>Conditions:</b> cannot collect tax from customers, cannot take ITC, must issue a bill of supply, and must display 'composition taxable person' at the place of business.

📝 <b>Exam tip:</b> The turnover limit is checked on ALL-India aggregate turnover under the same PAN – not State-wise.

<b>[Group 2]</b>
🧠 <b>CONCEPT OF THE DAY – Group 2</b>
📘 <b>Financial Management</b>

<b>Dividend decision – Walter and Gordon models</b>

• <b>Walter:</b> P = [D + (r ÷ Ke) × (E − D)] ÷ Ke, where r = return on investment and Ke = cost of equity.
• <b>Walter's rule:</b> r &gt; Ke (growth firm) → 0% payout is best; r &lt; Ke (declining firm) → 100% payout is best; r = Ke → payout does not matter.
• <b>Gordon:</b> P0 = E(1 − b) ÷ (Ke − br), where b = retention ratio and g = br.
• <b>Bird-in-hand</b> (Gordon): investors value a certain dividend today more than uncertain future gains, so Ke rises with retention.
• <b>MM approach:</b> in perfect markets, dividend policy does not affect the value of the firm.

📝 <b>Exam tip:</b> In Walter questions, find r and Ke first – the optimal payout is decided before any calculation of P.
